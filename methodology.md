# Methodology

## Research model

1. First principles — foundational political and philosophical questions.
2. Policy domains — concrete areas where principles have practical consequences.
3. Evidence and analysis — historical and empirical evidence, competing arguments, and documented political positions.
4. Personal position — provisional conclusions, policies, unresolved questions, and revisions.

## Statement types

- **FACT:** externally verifiable proposition.
- **CLAIM:** something asserted by a source or political actor.
- **INTERPRETATION:** analytical inference.
- **NORMATIVE JUDGEMENT:** value-based conclusion.

## Sources

Prefer primary sources for legislation, official documents, party manifestos, government statistics, official statements, court judgments, and parliamentary records. Use secondary sources for context, criticism, academic analysis, and synthesis.

## Versioning

The repository is the historical record. Significant changes in the owner's position should also be reflected in `CHANGELOG.md` and, where appropriate, `08-archive/`.

## Neutral research

When describing political parties or actors, distinguish documented facts from interpretation. Comparisons should describe differences in evidence, policies, assumptions, and consequences without substituting an external political preference for the owner's judgement.
